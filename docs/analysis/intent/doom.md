# DOOM (1993) - design-intent brief

## 1 Design pillars
- Speed and brutality over story: Romero wanted the game "more brutal and fast" than Wolfenstein, and Carmack put fast continuous play and technical innovation ahead of narrative. [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doom_(1993_video_game)]
- Drop the design document: Tom Hall's story-heavy "Doom Bible" was rejected and the team shipped without a design document. [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Development_of_Doom]
- Technology as the feature: the engine (any wall angle/height, distance-based lighting) was the showcase, and levels were meant to show it off rather than be boxy military bases. [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Development_of_Doom]
- Cut everything that slows the player: arcade score, lives, inventory, a secondary shield and a complex UI were gradually removed. [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Development_of_Doom]
- Nightmarish enemies and horror/action tone (Evil Dead II meets Aliens, tech vs demons). [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doom_(1993_video_game)]
- Multiplayer as a first-class mode, with deathmatch inspired by fighting games (Street Fighter II, Fatal Fury, Art of Fighting). [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Development_of_Doom]

## 2 Why players loved it
- Constant, readable action: enemies move toward you on sight or sound and often arrive in groups, so the loop is simple and intense. [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doom_(1993_video_game)]
- Distinct weapon fantasy ladder: pistol, shotgun, chainsaw, plasma rifle, BFG 9000, plus power-ups such as berserk, invisibility, invulnerability. [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doom_(1993_video_game)]
- Spaces that reward exploration: non-linear, horseshoe-shaped rooms and secret areas on every level. [https://www.mcvuk.com/development/video-john-romero-reveals-level-design-secrets-while-playing-doom]
- Deathmatch (2-4 players) and co-op gave endless replay; Romero coined "deathmatch". [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doom_(1993_video_game)]
- Free first episode removed the entry barrier; the first episode overwhelmed a university FTP server at launch, and about 20 million downloads were reported by June 1996. [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doom_(1993_video_game)]
- Critics praised single-player intensity, multiplayer, 3D graphics and atmosphere. [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doom_(1993_video_game)]
- Network popularity became a social phenomenon: Carnegie-Mellon posted a "do not play Doom in network mode" notice, and Intel banned it from its network. [https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/article/joyriding-with-the-masters-of-doom-a-tale-of-two-video-game-programmers/]
- A durable modding/WAD community followed (fan episodes such as Sigil, released by Romero). [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doom_(1993_video_game)]

## 3 Pacing and progression feel
- Structure: three episodes of eight levels, a fourth added in The Ultimate Doom (1995); five difficulty levels change enemy count, damage, and on the hardest setting speed. [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doom_(1993_video_game)]
- First hour: the first episode is the free demo, and Romero's rule is "finish the first level last" so that the opening uses everything learned in development. [https://www.mcvuk.com/development/video-john-romero-reveals-level-design-secrets-while-playing-doom]
- Romero's revised rules: open a level with something interesting or impressive, match the opening to its purpose (teach or scare), reuse areas so revisits reinforce understanding, and contrast light, sound and action. [https://doomwiki.org/wiki/Tips_for_creating_good_WADs]
- Romero's earlier rules also stress contrast between cramped and open spaces, multiple secrets per level and repeat passes past landmarks. [https://doomwiki.org/wiki/Tips_for_creating_good_WADs]
- Method: he builds from a theme, plans the progression of enemies, weapons and mechanics, and tests constantly (often within 10 minutes of the last change, hundreds of plays per level). [https://bethesda.net/it-IT/news/nods-to-mods-interview-john-romero]
- Mid/late game: progression is carried by new weapons, new enemy mixes and resource management of ammo, health and armor across levels, not by character stats. [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doom_(1993_video_game)]
- Reward pacing in the shareware model: episode 1 free, later episodes paid. [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doom_(1993_video_game)]

## 4 Signature features and systems
- 2.5D engine with billboarded sprite enemies, palette-based lighting and later BSP visibility. [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doom_(1993_video_game)]
- Shareware distribution, with retailers allowed to sell episode 1 at any price and id earning up to 85% on direct sales. [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doom_(1993_video_game)]
- Networked deathmatch/co-op, added by Carmack about a month before release; four-player online arrived via DWANGO about a year later. [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doom_(1993_video_game)]
- Cheat codes and open modding (WAD files, source later published). [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doom_(1993_video_game)]
- Monsters built from clay models photographed and converted to sprites; weapons from toy parts. [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Development_of_Doom]

## 5 Known flaws and regrets
- Engine limits: no vertically stacked areas; a single seamless world proved infeasible and forced a design rewrite. [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Development_of_Doom]
- Original Q3 1993 ship target missed; Hall fired in July 1993, Petersen hired about ten weeks before release. [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doom_(1993_video_game)]
- Edge criticised the shooting as shallow. [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doom_(1993_video_game)]
- Multiplayer initially worked only on LANs. [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doom_(1993_video_game)]
- Violence controversy, Germany's "harmful to minors" listing (rescinded 2011), and the post-Columbine blame. [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doom_(1993_video_game)]
- Weak ports (3DO, Saturn). [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doom_(1993_video_game)]
- Story-less design is a deliberate trade, not an accident; Carmack compared story in games to story in adult films, "expected but not important" (paraphrase of widely reported remark). [https://www.doomwiki.org/wiki/Doom_Bible]

## 6 What Frankendom should take
1. Zero-friction start and a free first slice. Make the first zone and first duel playable in seconds, with no account wall, and treat it as the shareware episode. SOURCED (shareware success) [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doom_(1993_video_game)]; the browser application is INFERRED.
2. Cut UI and systems that slow a fight. Id removed lives, inventory and a complex UI to protect pace; keep Frankendom's HUD minimal in duels. SOURCED [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Development_of_Doom]
3. Duels should be fighting-game-inspired. Deathmatch itself came from Street Fighter-style play; a duel-first design is consistent with the original intent. SOURCED [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Development_of_Doom]
4. Many-vs-one fights: enemies that close in on sight or sound, in groups, with readable weapon and enemy roles, are the core Doom pressure loop; tune the mob by difficulty level (count, damage, speed). Simple AI plus numbers is the recipe. SOURCED for the mechanism [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doom_(1993_video_game)]; application to Frankendom INFERRED.
5. Zone design: contrast (open/cramped, light/dark), revisitable landmarks, secrets in every zone, a strong opening beat, and build the entry zone last. SOURCED [https://doomwiki.org/wiki/Tips_for_creating_good_WADs]; [https://www.mcvuk.com/development/video-john-romero-reveals-level-design-secrets-while-playing-doom]
6. Ship editable content and tools (WADs) so the community extends zones and loot. SOURCED that the modding scene sustained Doom [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doom_(1993_video_game)]; MMO relevance INFERRED.
